# Eleven agent sidecar

The sidecar is the backend worker behind Eleven. It drafts the options the person hears (PLAN row 3.4), previews and sends form errands (row 4.2), and places consented calls. It runs on a laptop that is not the EEG laptop, connects to the SpacetimeDB module as the Agent role, and never acts on its own: a form or a call leaves the room only after the module records a fresh CONFIRM (double jaw clench) on the exact read-back the person heard (PLAN D4, D11).

Stephen's side owns the backend, this sidecar included (PLAN D14). Shashank owns the headset and the decoder in `switch/`, which reach the backend only through the SpacetimeDB contract in [docs/spacetime.md](../docs/spacetime.md). The product is Eleven; code identifiers keep `say-when` (PLAN D13), so the package is `@say-when/agent`.

Nothing here reads thoughts. The jaw clench is a muscle signal; the imagined squeezes are the brain test in `docs/brain-mode-test.md`.

## What it does

The sidecar watches three views of the module and reacts to them (docs/spacetime.md 7.4). Since module v2 (docs/spacetime.md section 14) it also watches `agent_consent`, the memory and web search consent it works under now, and it reads the public `device_status` table to tell whether it is paired. It no longer reads `board_calls`: `call_queue` carries each call's CONFIRM time.

It writes no `speak`, `listen` or `select` step. The module writes one speak step per read under its own keys, and refuses agent events under the prefixes it writes (`listen:`, `q<n>:`, `e<n>:`, `c<n>:`, `s<n>:`); the sidecar's keys start with the job id, and it writes only `search`, `retrieve`, `plan` and `errand` steps.

**Options job** (`agent_queue`, kind Options), for a partner question or a menu item the person picked:

0. **Yes or no fast path** (`AGENT_YES_NO_FAST`, on by default; `src/fastpath.ts`). A partner question that is clearly yes or no gets two stock options at once, `Yes.` then `No.` (replies, provenance `stock`), published final with `answer_mode` `yes_no`: no memory, no search, no Claude. In yes_no mode the station plays only the stock prompt "Yes or no?" and YES picks rank 1, NO rank 2, so the word said aloud is exactly the one the person chose. The rule is deterministic and conservative: the question begins with one of do, does, did, is, are, was, were, can, could, will, would, should, shall, have, has, had, may, might; offers no choice ("or", "either", "versus", "vs", "between", "rather", choose, pick) and has no comma, slash, ampersand or hyphen (a list such as "mild/moderate/severe" or "tea, coffee, juice"; a comma that only names someone, "Are you okay, Sam?", goes to the planner too); has no wh-word anywhere (what, when, where, which, who, whom, whose, why, how, whether, if); does not ask what the person knows or remembers (know, remember, recall: "Do you know the doctor's name?" asks for the name); does not leave the need open (anything, something, someone, anyone: "Do you need anything?" can become an errand option); names no errand or call verb in any form (call, phone, text, message, email, send, book, order, remind, schedule, buy, fill, submit, request, ask, tell, get, bring, fetch, contact, notify, reserve, pay, deliver), because a stock Yes would only say "Yes." and do nothing; is not a request ("can/could/would/will you" followed by anything other than hear, see, feel, like, want, need or understand, so "Could you breathe in for me?", "Could you drink the water?", "Could you move my chair?", "Will you come with us?" and "Could you be more specific?" and "Could you hand me the cup?" and "Would you mind" go to the planner while "Can you hear me?" stays fast); is not an offer to act ("want (me) to", "like (me) to", "need (me) to", "able, willing, ready, going or trying to", or "should/shall/can/could/may/will I or we", followed by a verb other than those or go, sit, rest, stay, wait, stand or lie (no verb that acts on a thing or a person, and no instruction: move, come, read, watch, play, try, walk, talk, listen, eat, drink, swallow, sleep, breathe; "Did you sleep well?" and "Did you eat lunch?" still stay fast), so "Do you want me to open the window?" goes to the planner while "Do you want to go outside?" and "May I sit here?" stay fast); asks one thing (no and, but, then, also or plus: "Can you hear me and say your name?" goes to the planner); asks for no words or showing (mind, describe, explain, say, spell, show, repeat, share, point); is one sentence; does not end with "." or "!"; and, after do, does, did or a modal (can, could, will, would, should, shall, may, might), has a pronoun subject and a main verb from a short plain list (want, like, need, feel, hear, see, sleep, eat, drink, go, sit, rest, hurt, be and a few more), and after have, has or had a pronoun subject and a plain participle (eaten, slept, heard, seen, been, met and a few more). Also: at most 9 words; every "to" followed by a verb is checked against the plain list ("Do you care to elaborate?", "going to the store"); after do, a modal or have, at most 4 words follow the main verb and none of them is a particle such as up, out, about or back ("Can you come up with one?"); "is there" and "are there" count as open; and after is or are and a pronoun, a verb in -ing must be a plain one (feeling, coming, sleeping and a few more). Last and strictest: **every word must come from a closed list** in `src/fastpath.ts` (the auxiliaries, pronouns and small function words, the plain verbs, and words for how the person feels and what is around them, such as tired, cold, okay, water, lunch, pharmacy), except names after is, are, was or were ("Is Sam here?", "Is the Berkeley Public Library open on Sunday?"), and a plain verb after the main one may only come right after "to" ("Do you want to go outside?" stays fast, "Can you come read this?" does not). So "Did you finish the soup?", "Does your back hurt?", "Are you thinking of a word?", "Have you decided on lunch?", "Can you come help me?" and even "Is the soup spicy?" go to the planner, which can still answer yes or no; a question sent there without need costs a few seconds, a stock answer to a question that needed a choice, an action or words costs the person their answer. Contractions ("Don't", "Isn't") and menu turns go the normal way, and so does every question while a call about a recent errand is possible (an errand sent within the call window, a Dialer and a consented contact): only the planner sees that errand and the contacts, so only it can offer the call. The smoke tag `[yes_no]` always takes it. The decision and its reason code are logged for every partner question. Claude is not asked to check it: the module ends an Options job only with a final publish, so a check that agreed would have to publish the same two options again as a second version, which the station switches to at the end of the pass with a fresh set of passes.
1. **Memory first.** Moss recalls the person's own past replies to this partner and their preferences, locally, in milliseconds.
2. **Search only when needed (D8).** `classifyQuestion` decides on the laptop whether the question needs outside facts. "Are you tired?" never waits on a search. Menu turns never search.
3. **Search.** RAG Web Browser, or the Google Maps Scraper near the venue for place questions when `AGENT_PLACES_NEAR` is set. The search has its own deadline (20 s by default) so it stays inside the module's 45 s job lease, and the client does not retry: the question is sent once, right after the sidecar read the web search consent. While it runs, the person's own past replies are published as early options (when at least two clear the score floor and the question does not read like yes or no).
   - **Draft first** (`AGENT_DRAFT_FIRST`, **off** by default; see its limit at the end of this item). When nothing was published early, Claude writes a draft while the search runs, from memory and the conversation only (the prompt says a search is still running and not to guess facts; no call options, because a call option makes the Dialer prepare a request). The draft is published with `final = false`. The module scans the newest version, but a version published while the person hears options waits as `pending_version` until the end of that pass and is switched in with the `more_options` chime (spacetime/src/agent.ts `publishOptions`, clock.ts `endPass`; the module test "a newer version published while reading waits for the end of the pass"), so options never change during a read, and a pick resolves against the version being read, with `heard_option_id` checked fail closed (gesture.ts `resolveHeard`). When the search brings web passages, the options with them are planned and published as the final version. When it brings nothing (failed, timed out, no rows, web consent withdrawn), the draft's own options are published again as the final version (`plan ok draft kept`), with no second plan, unless the row carries a recent errand: the draft never saw it or the contacts, so the normal plan runs and can offer a call about it. A draft that comes back yes or no while a call about a recent errand is possible is not published at all (the normal plan follows and can offer the call); otherwise a yes or no draft is final at once and the search is stopped (`search ok search stopped`): in yes_no mode the person hears only "Yes or no?", so a second version would change nothing they hear and would only start the prompt over. A draft whose plan fails, whose publish is refused or whose memory consent was withdrawn is not published, and the job goes on to the final options. The cost: web options now arrive at the end of the draft's pass, not as the first options. **The limit, and why it is off by default** (found by the second outside review): when the draft's passes run out with no pick, the module ends the question as No pick and cancels the Options job (spacetime/src/clock.ts `endPass`, then `finishScan`, which calls `cancelJobs`), even though the final plan with web results is still on its way; the sidecar cannot see the scan to hold it open. With a two-option draft (two passes of about 12 s) and a slow search, the researched options can be lost. Making it safe needs a module change, for example `endPass` waiting in Preparing while an Options job for the question is still Claimed after a non-final publish. Turn it on only where that loss is acceptable.
4. **Retrieve.** The rows are chunked into the Moss web index for this question, the passages that match come back, and they are deleted once the question is answered. If Moss is closed, the planner reads the top rows directly. Every Moss call has its own deadline (recall 3 s, index 10 s, search 3 s, rank 3 s, write-back 5 s, forget 5 s), so a stuck call degrades that step instead of holding the job.
5. **Plan.** Claude writes up to four short options in the person's voice, each with its own tone, its kind (reply, errand or call) and the sources it used. A yes or no question gets exactly two (yes first). The reply is checked strictly (see "The planner").
6. **Publish.** Options are ordered by memory affinity (moss `rankOptions`; yes or no answers keep their order) and written with `agent_publish_options`.

**Errand preview job** (kind ErrandPreview, made when the person says YES to an errand option): the form-errand Actor fills the form and reads every value back off the page. The read-back text and the 16-character digest go to the errand row with `agent_attach_preview`. Nothing is sent.

**Errand submit** (`errand_queue`): when the module shows an errand Confirmed, the sidecar checks that the confirmed digest, form address and fields are exactly the ones it previewed. Then `claim_errand`, one submit, and `complete_errand`. A digest it did not preview is refused (claimed, then completed as `refused` with `digest_mismatch`, nothing sent). An errand this sidecar never previewed is left alone. A submit whose outcome is unclear is recorded as `unknown`, which the module shows as "It may have been sent. Check before trying again." A submit is never retried. A claim is tried again only when it provably never reached the module (the connection was down for every try), at most three rounds; a claim that may have landed is never sent again.

**Calls** (`call_queue`, stretch): a call option is prepared by the Cartesia Dialer when the planner offers it, so the read-back and digest exist at the pick. When the module shows the call Confirmed, the sidecar claims it and calls `dialer.place(requestId, digest, confirmedAtMs)` with the module's CONFIRM time (the Dialer re-checks consent, the do-not-call list and calling hours). Ringing, connected, transcript lines (numbers already masked) and the end are reported in order. STOP, HEADSET_OFF, the idle pause or an operator set `cancel_requested`, and the sidecar hangs up (or never dials). One call at a time: when `claim_call` answers `line busy` (another call is live), the call waits for the next change to the views and is claimed again then, when the freshness checks decide whether it may still be placed. A call whose dial lease lapsed in the module is hung up too: it stays in `call_queue` as Failed with `cancel_requested`, because it may still be on the line. Every final report carries the Dialer's sentence for how the call ended in `reason` (for example "Leon did not answer."; a STOP that arrived while the claim was out gives "Cancelled. No call was made."). Since module v2 the module keeps it for ended calls too, privately, on the board only while the room shares text. When the Dialer puts the contact on hold (`held`: a possible refusal was heard, the end is unknown, or its hold file could not be written), that sentence ends "Calls to <name> are on hold until someone checks.", and a call that otherwise ended normally also carries `opt_out_hold` or `hold_file_error`; the module shows either as `board_calls.held`. `test/contract.test.ts` checks every held outcome against the module's own hold codes and sentence. One CONFIRM, one call.

A call must follow its CONFIRM closely, and three places check it:

1. **The module.** `claim_call` refuses a CONFIRM 12 s old or older with `confirm expired` (module v2), checked before `line busy`. The sidecar treats that refusal as final: the call is never claimed again or dialed, and the module stops it itself with `confirm_expired` when its timer runs, so the sidecar sends no report for it. This also ends a call that waited for the line too long. A test reads the module's 12 s from `spacetime/src/consent.ts`.
2. **The sidecar**, right before the dial, after the claim. It checks two clocks and dials only if both say the CONFIRM is recent:
   - The module's clock: the CONFIRM's time from `call_queue.confirmed_at` must be at most 12 s old by the laptop's clock. If it is unknown (bindings older than module v2 do not carry it), or more than 5 s in the future (the two clocks disagree), the call is refused with `confirm_time_unknown`.
   - The sidecar's own clock: at most 10 s since it first saw the call Confirmed. A Confirmed call that was already there when the views were loaded (startup or a reconnect) is of unknown age.

   A call that fails either check is claimed and reported refused (`confirm_expired`, "Too much time passed after you confirmed, so I did not call. Ask again if you still want the call."), and never dialed. Claims are tried again by the same rule as errands.
3. **The Dialer**, which is given the CONFIRM's time as `place`'s third argument for its own 30 s window.

A placed call is watched too. After `place` the Dialer still checks its Cartesia agent and voice (requests with 10 s and 15 s timeouts) before it dials. If the Dialer has not dialed (no call id yet) by 30 s after the CONFIRM, less the 5 s allowed for clock difference, the sidecar cancels the call; the Dialer's last check before its outbound request then stops it, and the person hears the same `confirm_expired` sentence (with the Dialer's hold sentence when it put the contact on hold). A test reads the Dialer's 30 s window from `cartesia/src/call.ts`.

**Remember job**: the reply that was said aloud is written into Moss for that partner, so the next question already knows it.

**Leases.** A job that leaves the sidecar while it runs (cancelled, superseded, or requeued after its lease lapsed) is aborted, and the module refuses anything it still writes as a stale attempt. A job that does not stop within 5 s gives up its slot, so the requeued job can be claimed again at once. If a claim lands at a later attempt than the sidecar computed (another claim and a lapsed lease came first), the sidecar uses the attempt the module shows, also when the claim's reply was lost and the view had to be read to find out. Every `agent_progress` renews the lease, also one the module already has, so a step that waits on something slow (a planner retry, a search, a form preview) sends its own `started` event again every 10 s while it is open: no new rows, and the lease does not run out under work that is still going.

## Run it

You need:

- Node 22.
- `apify/client`, `moss` and `cartesia` built (`npm run build` in each): this package depends on their `dist/` through `file:` links.
- The module's generated bindings in `spacetime/bindings/agent`, and `npm ci` done in `spacetime/` (the bindings import `spacetimedb` from there). Generate them with `spacetime generate --no-config --lang typescript --out-dir spacetime/bindings/agent --module-path spacetime`.
- An Agent identity paired with the database, and the module's consent rows for web search, memory and each errand form (docs/spacetime.md 9.4).

In `agent/`:

```bash
npm ci
npm run check    # every setting by name, set or not, and whether the bindings are there. Never prints a value.
npm run pair     # once per database: prints the approve_pairing command for the owner, then waits for it
npm start        # runs the sidecar. Ctrl-C stops it cleanly; a second Ctrl-C exits at once.
npm run warm     # in a second terminal, right before a judge's first question (see "Warm")
```

`npm start` loads `agent/.env.local`, then the repo's `.env.local`, without overriding variables already set (`AGENT_ENV_FILES=off` skips both). It stops before connecting if a setting is malformed or there are no Anthropic credentials, and says which variable to fix.

### Keys from the macOS Keychain

`scripts/run-with-keychain.sh` runs the same commands with the keys read from the Keychain at run time, so no key sits in a file:

```bash
agent/scripts/run-with-keychain.sh check    # npm run check
agent/scripts/run-with-keychain.sh pair     # npm run pair
agent/scripts/run-with-keychain.sh          # npm start
agent/scripts/run-with-keychain.sh warm     # npm run warm, with the same keys and database
```

| Variable | Keychain service |
|---|---|
| `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` | `anthropic-api-key` |
| `APIFY_TOKEN` | `apify-token` |
| `MOSS_PROJECT_ID` | `moss-project-id` |
| `MOSS_PROJECT_KEY` | `moss-project-key` |
| `CARTESIA_API_KEY` | `cartesia-api-key` |

- Each key goes only into the sidecar's environment. Nothing is printed, logged or written to a file, and settings files are skipped (`AGENT_ENV_FILES=off`).
- Each item is read by service and account: `KEYCHAIN_ACCOUNT`, or your login name by default. Without the account, `security` returns the first item with that service; on the build Mac an older `anthropic-api-key` item (account `default`) is refused by Anthropic with 401.
- It refuses to start when an item is missing or empty, and names the variable (an empty Keychain item still exits 0, so it checks the length).
- It refuses any database whose name does not end in `-dev`. Pass `--allow-non-dev` to use `say-when-demo` on purpose.
- Calls are always off here (`AGENT_CALLS=off`): calls need consent confirmed under the Eleven name first.
- Defaults, each overridden by a value already in the environment: `SPACETIMEDB_DB=say-when-dev`, `SPACETIMEDB_URI` Maincloud, `AGENT_PLANNER_MODEL=claude-haiku-5-5`, `FORM_ERRAND_URL` the form-errand Actor's Standby URL, `AGENT_FORMS_FILE=agent/forms.example.json` (the Actor's built-in demo form, which sends nothing anywhere; grant `errand care_team_request` for it), `MOSS_SNAPSHOT_DIR=moss/.memory`, and `AGENT_TOKEN_FILE=~/.config/say-when/agent_token_<database>`, outside the repo and one per database.

### Warm

`npm run warm` gets the slow first steps out of the way before a judge's first question, and prints one line per step with its time (`warm moss ok 812 ms: ...`). It never prints a key, a token, a URL or any content, and exits 0 only when all three are ok:

- **moss.** Moss runs inside the sidecar and closes itself after 3 idle minutes, so opening it in the short-lived `warm` process would bill a second session and help nothing. `warm` finds the running sidecar by its pid file (`<AGENT_TOKEN_FILE>.pid`: the pid and a random nonce for this run, written once the sidecar can answer, removed when it stops). A pid left by a crash can name another process, which the signal would end, so before it signals `warm` confirms with `ps` and `lsof` that the process is alive, runs `src/main.ts run`, works in this package's `agent/` folder (as `run-with-keychain.sh` and `npm start` run it) and started before the pid file was written. Anything it cannot confirm is skipped (`warm moss not ok 0 ms: skipped: ...`) and nothing is sent. The sidecar then opens its Moss, or, when it is already open, makes one local read (a recall for a partner id no one has) so its idle clock starts again, bounded at 30 s. It logs the result and writes it, with the nonce, to `<AGENT_TOKEN_FILE>.warm`; `warm` waits at most 40 s for a status carrying the nonce from the pid file, and prints it. Both files are written to a new temp file (exclusive, never through a symlink, mode 600) and renamed into place; the write is refused when a symlink stands at the path or the folder belongs to another user or can be written by others, and neither file is ever read through a symlink. The 3 minutes then run again: warm within 3 minutes of the first question, or set `AGENT_MOSS_REOPEN=on`.
- **apify.** One `GET /` of the form-errand Actor's Standby URL (`FORM_ERRAND_URL`), with the token in the Authorization header and redirects refused. The Actor answers it with its description, and the request starts a container if none was up. No preview, no submit, no form. The public RAG Web Browser is not touched.
- **cartesia.** Mints one 60-second TTS access token with `CARTESIA_API_KEY` and drops it, to show the key and the API answer. The station's own tokens come from the module's `mint_voice_token`, which uses the module's copy of the key.

## Privacy rules it enforces

- Public events never carry the question, option text, memory text, form values, read-back text, names, phone numbers or URLs: only fixed labels, durations and numbers.
- Page text (in payloads and in the planner prompt) has phone numbers (seven digits or more, in any script, with any spaces, characters with the Unicode Dash property or invisible separators), keys and tokens masked; opening hours, prices and street numbers stay, because the planner needs them. This is a different rule from the Cartesia package's, which masks every number in call transcripts.
- Memory text never reaches SpacetimeDB, an event, a log or a screen. A memory the person marked shareable appears only as `sharedText`; any other shows as "Private memory".
- Memory text and the conversation go to the Anthropic API inside the planner prompt, and only when the job has memory consent. They never go into a search query: Apify gets the partner's question, or a short place word such as "pharmacy".
- Consent can be revoked while a job runs: the module then clears the job's web search or memory flag, cancels a Remember job, and shows it in `agent_consent`. A job uses memory or web search only while its own flag and `agent_consent` both allow it, and an `agent_consent` the sidecar cannot read (no row, or a row that does not match the contract) allows neither. The sidecar reads both again right before each use, after every wait that comes first (the recall, the search, the early memory options, each planner attempt including a retry, the memory hits shown beside the web hits, the memory ranking, the write into memory), so nothing goes out on a consent that was just withdrawn. A plan that relied on memory, the conversation, web passages or a memory ranking whose consent was withdrawn on the way (while Claude wrote it, while the ranking ran, while the plan step's event was written, or between publish tries) is not published: it is written again without them, three rounds at most. The check fails closed: while the connection is down or the subscription is being applied again, a revocation could be missing from the views, so neither consent holds; and a job attempt that is no longer this sidecar's (a later attempt took it) sends neither. An aborted Remember attempt writes nothing and leaves the job to the next attempt; a Remember job that was taken away (aborted, or cancelled, which is how a memory revocation shows) also forgets the kept conversation.
- The conversation with each partner (the last turns, used as planner context) is kept and sent only under memory consent. Whenever a job sees memory consent withdrawn (at the start or at any later check), or `agent_consent` shows it off (with or without a job running; each row is read as it arrives, so a revocation followed at once by a new grant still counts), or the views are current and `agent_consent` cannot be read, or the connection or the subscription is lost (a revocation during the gap, given again before the views come back, would leave no trace), every kept turn is forgotten.
- Read-back values go only to the errand row. Call transcripts arrive masked from the Dialer and go only to `call_line`. The Dialer's sentence for how a call ended names the contact and goes only to the call row's private `reason`. Phone numbers never leave the Dialer.
- The terminal log has fixed messages with ids, codes, counts and times.
- Startup checks print each setting's name and whether it is set, never its value. When the sidecar cannot start, it prints only messages written to be shown (its own, and the sibling packages' configuration errors, which name settings and positions); any other error is reported by its code or name, because Node's messages quote file paths and JSON errors quote pieces of the file.

`test/privacy.test.ts` runs a question, an errand and a call seeded with marker strings, then the failure paths with content in every error message, body and reason a service could send back, and checks each rule. The checks ignore case and punctuation (the way `label()` reshapes text), catch a secret by any of its distinctive words, and accept only the fixed label vocabulary and the known count keys. Negative controls show each check catches a reshaped or partial leak.

## The planner

- **Model:** `claude-haiku-5-5`, the current fast model, because the person is waiting with the headset on. `AGENT_PLANNER_MODEL` overrides it (for example `claude-opus-5-5`).
- **Call:** the Anthropic TypeScript SDK (`@anthropic-ai/sdk` 0.137.0, the last 0.x; 1.x moves to API version 2026-10-21, which this code has not been checked against), `client.beta.messages.create` with `output_config.format` built from a zod schema (structured output), `output_config.effort` (`low` by default), and the model's default adaptive thinking. No sampling parameters. The fixed system prompt is marked for caching; everything that varies is in the user message.
- **Stop reason first:** a refusal or a cut-off reply is its own error, read before the reply is parsed.
- **Fallback:** for `claude-opus-5-5`, `claude-opus-5`, `claude-fable-5-1` and `claude-sonnet-5-5` the request opts into the server-side refusal fallback (`fallbacks: "default"`). Claude Haiku 5.5 has none, so a refusal there fails the job.
- **Retries:** the SDK's own retries are off. One attempt plus at most one retry on a timeout, a network error, a 5xx, a 529 or a 429 with a short Retry-After. Everything else fails at once with a clear code (`planner_<code>` in `agent_fail`; the person hears "I could not write options. Please ask again.").
- **Strict checks** (`src/planner/validate.ts`): 1 to 4 options for a pick, exactly two replies for yes or no with yes first; distinct tones from neutral, calm, warm, sad, firm; at most one laugh; no duplicate text, no markup, at most 200 characters; every cited source was in the prompt; at least one reply (an errand or a call is never the only option); an errand only for an allowed form, with known field labels, valid choices and every required field (fixed values such as who it is from are filled by the sidecar); a call only to a consented contact while a request sent less than eight minutes ago can still be reported. Any broken rule fails the reply with the rule names, never its text.

## Swapping pieces

Everything outside the sidecar sits behind a small interface in `src/ports.ts` (search, memory, web index, form errands, Dialer, planner) and `src/db/db.ts` (the module). `src/wiring.ts` builds the real ones; the tests pass fakes. `MemoryDb` (`src/db/memory-db.ts`) applies the module's rules for the agent reducers, for tests and offline runs.

Every SpacetimeDB name the sidecar uses is in `src/contract.ts`. The bindings load at run time from `spacetime/bindings/agent` (`src/db/bindings.ts`), because the generated files use extensionless imports that run under tsx but do not type-check under this package's NodeNext settings. `npm run typecheck` also compiles `typecheck/bindings.check.ts` against them: it fails if the generated connection stops fitting the adapter, a reducer stops taking the sidecar's arguments, or a view row or an enum changes. `test/contract.test.ts` checks the same names against docs/spacetime.md, the module source and the generated files. After every `spacetime generate`, run `npm run typecheck` and `npm test` here.

## Known limits

- The digests this sidecar previewed are kept in memory. After a restart, an errand that was previewed before the restart and confirmed after it is left alone (the next question, a pause or the end of the session stops it).
- A sidecar that dies during a submit or a call leaves it to the module's leases: the submit becomes unknown_outcome, the call failed with result unknown. Nothing is resent.
- Run one sidecar per identity. Two processes sharing `.agent-token` look like one agent to the module.
- The module writes its own `plan ok` event when options are published; the sidecar writes one too, with the Claude time and token counts.
- Conversation history (the last turns with each partner) lives in process memory, only under memory consent, and is lost on restart.
- A call is placed only while the views are current; if the connection is down at the moment of the dial, the call is refused (`disconnected`, "I lost the connection, so I did not call. Ask again if you still want the call."), and the report lands once the connection is back.
- The sidecar's call freshness check compares the module's CONFIRM time with the laptop's clock, so it is only as good as the two clocks' agreement (both normally keep network time; a gap of more than 5 s refuses calls). The module's own 12 s claim window uses only the module's clock. The sidecar passes the CONFIRM time to `place`, but this package's tests use a fake Dialer: whether the Dialer measures its 30 s window from it is up to `cartesia/` (a Dialer that ignores it still fits the port). Until a Dialer checks it right before its outbound request, the sidecar's own deadline cancels a call that has not dialed in time; a cancel that lands while the Dialer's outbound request is already on its way cannot stop that request, and the Dialer then hangs up as soon as it has the call id, so the phone may ring briefly. Errands have no such window: a confirmed errand is submitted when its claim lands, and one confirmed before a restart is left alone (see the first limit).
