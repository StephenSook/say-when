# Brain mode: the test it must pass first

Written and committed before any measurement, so the bar cannot move after we see the numbers.

Brain mode is optional. It lets one wearer use an imagined right-fist squeeze as the CALL request. It is never the confirm: a call still needs the spoken read-back and a fresh SELECT. If the test below fails, CALL uses the eye hold, and the measured number goes in the README as an experiment.

## The rule

An imagined right-fist squeeze counts as a brain command for this wearer, today, only if all of these hold:

1. The headset has electrodes at C3 and C4. A Muse cannot do this; its sensors are at TP9, AF7, AF8 and TP10.
2. The decoder was trained only on an earlier calibration block of 40 to 60 cued trials.
3. The headset was then taken off and put back on.
4. 40 fresh trials followed: 20 imagery and 20 rest, in random order, with spoken cues, eyes on a fixed point, and hands flat and visible on the table.
5. Balanced accuracy was at least 80%: at least 16 of 20 right in each class. Any trial flagged as an artifact counts as a miss, never dropped.
6. In a control block (10 jaw clenches, 10 double blinks, 10 left and 10 right glances, 30 s of talking, 30 s with eyes closed, 60 s of idle listening) it fired zero times.
7. Jaw-muscle power at the temple sites and eye movement at the forehead sites did not differ systematically between imagery and rest trials.

## What a pass means

- Guessing gets about 20 of 40. Getting 32 or more of 40 by guessing has a probability of about 0.0001.
- Even 32 of 40 leaves a 95% confidence interval of about 64% to 91% for the true accuracy, so we report "32 of 40", not "80% accurate".
- Zero firings in about 60 control events still allows up to about 5% per event at 95% confidence.
- A pass covers this wearer, this headset, today, these conditions. It does not mean the headset reads intentions, and it says nothing about people with ALS, who in published work needed months of training to reach about 80%.

## How we talk about it

- Passed: "Imagining a fist squeeze is a trained brain command for [wearer] today: [N] of 40 fresh trials right, where guessing gets about 20, and zero firings while they clenched, blinked, talked and looked around."
- Failed: "We tested an imagined fist squeeze as a brain command. It scored [N] of 40, below the bar we wrote down before testing, so CALL uses an eye movement instead."
- Never say "thought commands", "mind control" or "it reads what they want".
